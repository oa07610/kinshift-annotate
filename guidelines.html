KINSHIFT guidelines

Annotation guidelines

Version 0.1, October 2026. Read all of this before you start. It takes about 10 minutes.

What KINSHIFT is

KINSHIFT tests whether AI language models know and can reason about family words in Pakistani languages: what you call your father's elder brother, your mother's sister's husband, your husband's younger brother, and so on. You are the expert. The words you give become the correct answers the models are checked against, so they must be the words real speakers of your language use.

The work, round by round

  1. Onboarding (about 1 hour): consent, a short form about you, these guidelines, a 5-question check.
  2. Kinship words (4 to 5 hours): give your language's words for 150 relatives.
  3. Later rounds (each 2 to 5 hours, November and December): correct question wording, check questions, and mark AI answers. The team will tell you when each opens.

Golden rules

  • Give the word you use at home. Not the word from a dictionary, a textbook, or the news, and not the Urdu or English word, unless that is what people really say. If there is a more formal word too, put it in Notes.
  • Use only your own knowledge. Do not use ChatGPT, Google, dictionaries, or ask others while answering. We need what you know. The page records time and pasted text so we can check quality.
  • Answer every relative on your own. Do not discuss answers with the other annotators. Differences between you are useful, not mistakes.
  • If you are not sure, say so. Pick "not sure" and explain in Notes. An honest "not sure" is far more useful than a guess.

Reading each relative

Each relative is written as a path from you, and drawn as a small chart (squares are men, circles are women, the diamond is you, the highlighted shape is the relative being asked about; a dashed line is a marriage).

  • "father's elder brother" means your father's brother who is older than your father. Elder and younger always compare with the person just before them in the path.
  • "elder sister" on its own means your sister who is older than you.
  • "mother's sister's husband" is married to your mother's sister. He is not related to your mother by blood.
  • When the path starts with husband, the speaker is a married woman. When it starts with wife, the speaker is a married man.
  • Assume ordinary families: no step-relatives, no adoption, and no marriages between relatives.

Filling each box

  • Talking ABOUT the relative: the word you use when telling someone else about them. In English, "my mother".
  • Talking TO the relative: what you call them to their face, only if different. In English you might say "Mum". Leave empty if it is the same word.
  • In your script: type in your language's own letters. Use the letter buttons at the bottom of the screen for letters your keyboard lacks. Do not add short-vowel marks unless people normally write them.
  • In Roman letters: spell it the way you would on WhatsApp. Put other common spellings in "Other Roman spellings", separated by commas.
  • If a woman says it: only if women use a different word for this relative than men do.
  • Same word as another relative: if your language uses one word for two relatives (for example, one word for two different aunts), write the word once in the first row and choose "same word as" in the other.
  • No single word: if people describe this relative with a phrase instead of one word, tick the box and write the phrase.
  • Elder / younger words: if people add a word meaning "big" or "small" to show age, write that word here, not inside the main word.
  • Other regions or communities: words you know are used elsewhere, with where.

Saving and sending your work

  • Answers save automatically in this browser. Use the same browser on the same device each time.
  • Press Export my work after every batch and send the file to the team. If your browser data is cleared, only the exported file survives.
  • If you change device or browser, use Restore from a file with your last export first.
  • Questions or problems: contact the team. Do not ask other annotators about answers.
