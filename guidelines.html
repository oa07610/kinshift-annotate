Annotation guidelines

Version 0.3, October 2026. About 10 minutes to read. Please read all of it before you start.

What this is

KINSHIFT tests whether AI models know the family words of Pakistani languages: what you call your father's elder brother, your mother's sister's husband, your husband's younger brother, and so on. You are the expert. The words you give become the correct answers the AI is checked against, so they must be the words real speakers use.

What you will do

  1. Onboarding, about 20 minutes: consent, four questions about you, and a short check.
  2. Kinship words, about 3 hours in total: one relative per screen, 150 relatives in 23 small batches. Stop and continue whenever you like.
  3. Later, about 1 to 2 hours, only if we need you: resolving disagreements (reviewers only) or checking that a sample of questions reads naturally. The team will tell you when.

Four rules

  • Give the word you use at home, not a dictionary, textbook or news word, and not the Urdu or English word unless that is what people really say. If a formal word also exists, add it under Notes.
  • Use only your own knowledge. Do not use ChatGPT, Google or dictionaries, and do not ask others while answering. The page records time and pasted text.
  • Work on your own. Do not discuss answers with other annotators. Differences between you are useful, not mistakes.
  • If you are not sure, say so. Choose "Not sure" and explain in Notes. An honest "not sure" is far more useful than a guess.

Reading each relative

Each relative is a path from you, shown as words and as a small chart. In the chart the diamond is you, squares are men, circles are women, the blue shape is the relative being asked about, and a dashed line is a marriage.

  • "father's elder brother" is your father's brother who is older than your father. Elder and younger always compare with the person just before them in the path.
  • "elder sister" on its own is your sister who is older than you.
  • "mother's sister's husband" is married into the family; he is not your mother's blood relative.
  • A path starting with husband means the speaker is a married woman; one starting with wife means a married man.
  • Assume ordinary families: no step-relatives, no adoption, no marriages between relatives.

Answering

Each relative has one main question, shown with a sentence to help: "This is my ___." Fill in the blank with the words you would use.

  • Write every word people use for this relative where you grew up, the most common first. For example, if your family says both "abbu" and "baba" for father, write both. Use + another word to add more, and ↑ most common to move a word to the top.
  • Each word in your script and in Roman letters. Type the Roman spelling the way you would on WhatsApp; if people spell it several ways, separate them with commas (abbu, abbo). Your script is required for the first word only.
  • Calling them. For elders there is a second sentence: "___, come and eat!" Only fill it in if you would call them something different, for example "chachu" when you call him but "chacha" when you talk about him. Leave it empty if it is the same word.
  • Words from other places (another city, another community) and formal or textbook words go in the notes, not in the list. If women say it differently from men, write both in the notes.
  • Leave out "jee" or "ji" unless it is really part of the word.
  • If people describe this relative with a few words instead of one, just write that phrase as the word.
  • Not sure? Tick I'm not sure about this one and say why in the notes.

Signing in and saving

  • Sign in by typing your name exactly as the team sent it to you.
  • Your answers save in this browser as you type, and are sent to the team automatically after each batch and every few minutes. You can also press Submit now at any time.
  • If you sign in on another phone or computer, your submitted work comes back automatically.
  • If the page says it could not reach the server, keep working: nothing is lost, and it will send later.
  • Questions or problems: contact the team, not the other annotators.
